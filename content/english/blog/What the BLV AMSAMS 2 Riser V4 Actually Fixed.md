Most upgrades solve one thing. You identify a problem, find a mod that addresses it, print it, install it, move on.

The BLV AMS / AMS 2 Riser v4, designed by benlevi, is not that kind of upgrade. It's the rare mod where you install it for one reason and immediately realize it was already solving three other problems you'd quietly accepted as facts of life.

Here are the four problems it fixed — and exactly how it fixed each one.

## Problem 1: A PTFE Tube Worn Flat After 5,000 Hours

During routine maintenance on an X1C with 5,000 hours of print time, the inspection revealed something that should get the attention of any high-volume Bambu Lab user: the PTFE tube had been physically worn flat.

The cause is mechanical and cumulative. With the AMS sitting directly on top of the printer, the PTFE tube's routing creates consistent contact against the top glass panel. Every print cycle, every filament change, every small vibration — it's all friction on the same contact point. Over thousands of hours, that friction deforms the tube from round to flat, degrading filament feed consistency and eventually forcing a full PTFE tube replacement along with AMS disassembly.

### What the Riser does: 
 It elevates the AMS, raising the top glass cover and creating genuine clearance between the PTFE tube and the panel. The tube no longer contacts the lid during operation. The friction point is eliminated. For a printer with serious hours on it — or one that's heading that direction — this is preventive maintenance built into the upgrade itself.


## Problem 2: Time-Lapse Footage Too Dark to Use

The P1S is an enclosed printer by design, and that enclosure is a meaningful feature for high-temperature materials and chamber temperature control. But it creates a real problem for anyone using the internal camera: the sealed environment limits the light available for photography, and the results show. Time-lapse footage shot through the P1S camera is chronically underexposed — dark, flat, and largely unusable for content or documentation purposes.

External lighting rigs solve this, but they're a workaround for a problem that lives inside the machine.

### What the Riser does:
 Two things. First, it raises the top cover, allowing it to be opened during lower-temperature prints — letting ambient light into the chamber. Second, and more significantly, the Riser includes dedicated mounting positions for LED light strips along the interior. Installed LED strips illuminate the print chamber directly and consistently, producing footage that's bright, evenly lit, and genuinely usable. This is a proper fix for the lighting problem, not a workaround — the light source moves with the printer's physical footprint.

## Problem 3: Constantly Searching for the Scraper

This is the smallest problem on the list by any objective measure, and the one that causes the most low-grade daily friction.

Print finishes. You need the scraper to remove support structures and flow calibration lines from the build plate. The scraper is not on the printer. It's not next to the printer. It's somewhere nearby, placed down between prints without a designated home. You find it, do the work, put it back somewhere, and the cycle resets.

Multiply this by the number of print cycles in a day across an active setup, and it adds up to a meaningful amount of wasted time and mild frustration.

### What the Riser does:
 It integrates a drawer for tools and accessories directly into the printer's physical footprint, along with dedicated slots for build plates, magnetic attachments, and adapters — including support for third-party accessories. The scraper lives in the drawer. The drawer is part of the printer. There's no separate decision about where to put things, because the place for things is already built in. Workspace organization becomes structural rather than habitual.

## Problem 4: Heat Creep During PLA Prints With the Door Closed
PLA is one of the most common 3D printing materials and one of the most problematic to run on an enclosed printer like the P1S or X1C. The enclosed chamber retains heat that PLA's lower thermal requirements don't call for — the result is heat creep, where elevated temperatures climb up into the cold zone of the hotend, cause partial melting above the heat break, and eventually produce a clog that stops the print entirely.

The standard workaround is to print PLA with the top door open. But with the AMS mounted directly on top, opening the door means physically removing or repositioning the AMS first. It's not a practical option mid-session.

### What the Riser does: 
 By elevating the AMS, it creates enough clearance to open the top lid fully without dismounting anything. PLA printing with an open lid becomes a normal operating mode — no disassembly, no workaround. The airflow that PLA printing requires is available on demand, and heat creep stops being a print failure risk for your PLA jobs.


## The Broader Case for This Upgrade
What makes the BLV Riser V4 compelling isn't that it solves four problems — it's that the four problems it solves are genuinely different in kind. PTFE wear is a long-term mechanical issue. Time-lapse quality is a content production issue. Scraper organization is a workflow issue. PLA heat creep is a material compatibility issue. A single structural intervention — raising the AMS and the top panel — turns out to be the common thread connecting all four.

The V4 is also confirmed compatible with the AMS HT, which means it carries forward if you upgrade your material handling system. That's meaningful for an upgrade that modifies your printer's physical configuration — you want to know it won't become obsolete with the next hardware generation.

## Who Should Print This 
Any X1C or P1S user past 1,500–2,000 hours who hasn't inspected their PTFE tube recently

Makers who document their prints or create time-lapse content and want usable footage without external lighting

Anyone running a multi-material setup with regular filament changes who wants their workspace organized around the machine

Builders who print PLA regularly and have been working around the heat creep issue with manual workarounds

Users planning an AMS HT upgrade who want their current physical setup to remain compatible
