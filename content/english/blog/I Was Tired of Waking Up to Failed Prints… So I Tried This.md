## 1. What Problem Does It Actually Solve?
The root cause of most AMS loading failures comes down to a few culprits: filament tangles, bent or deformed filament tails, and the compounding damage those cause to your internal PTFE tube over time.

Every failed load isn't just an inconvenience — it's wear on your machine. Repeated jams can force you to fully disassemble your AMS, clear the blockage, and replace the PTFE tube. That's time, money, and frustration that adds up fast.

The Snag Cutter addresses this at the source by cleanly cutting the filament tail before it has a chance to cause a jam.

## 2. How It Works 
The V5.0 features a refined two-step slide mechanism that triggers two distinct sensitivity levels for the cut — a thoughtful design detail that gives the system more nuance than a simple snap-cut approach. The result is a more reliable cut across different filament types and spool conditions.

The snap-fit mounting system makes installation genuinely tool-free. It locks in securely once seated, but can be removed without damage if you need to swap blades or make adjustments. The blade housing uses cylindrical pins with a slight draft angle on the receiving holes — a small engineering choice that makes a real difference when you're assembling or disassembling the unit.

Hexavalent provides pre-configured slicing files where all parts fit on a single plate, ready to print as a complete AMS build. That kind of thoughtful packaging matters.

## 3. Real-World Install Notes 

A few things worth knowing going in:

Blade orientation matters. It's easy to install the wrong blade length on the first attempt. The good news: the snap-fit design means you can disassemble and correct it without any damage.

Material can affect print quality. Printing the standard parts set in ABS produced some minor defects in the cylindrical sections of two arm components (the Short Blade and Long Blade normal-gap variants). Other blades printed cleanly. If you're seeing similar issues, consider trying a different material or print profile.

Spool alignment is a factor. One AMS unit had a cutting failure on first use due to a tilted filament spool. Repositioning the spool resolved it completely — it didn't recur.

Shelf clearance is tight. Depending on your setup, limited shelf height can make initial filament installation a little fiddly. It's workable, but worth knowing ahead of time.

## 4. Who Should Add This to Their Setup?
If you run long or overnight prints, use multi-color setups with frequent filament changes, or have already dealt with PTFE tube damage from jams — this upgrade is a direct answer to your pain points. The installation is low-risk, the design is well-documented, and the failure modes are recoverable.

If you're newer to AMS printing and haven't hit filament jam issues yet, it's still a worthwhile preventive addition. It's easier to install this before you need it than after you've already had to disassemble your AMS at midnight.


## FAQ
### Why does the Bambu AMS fail to load filament?
Common causes include:

bent filament tips
filament snagging
spool resistance
worn PTFE tubes
tangled filament

### Can filament jams damage AMS PTFE tubes?
Yes. Repeated jams can deform or wear the internal PTFE tube over time, sometimes requiring disassembly and replacement.

### Is the AMS Snag Cutter easy to install?
Yes. The module uses a snap-fit style installation and can be removed easily for blade adjustments or maintenance.
